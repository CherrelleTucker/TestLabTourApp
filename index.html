🛡️ Before you go

Arrival: Arrive at the time on your agenda and park in the visitor lots indicated below. Call your Tour Guide (listed on your agenda) when you arrive. If the Tour Guide knows there is a tour but doesn't see anyone at the doors, they will attempt to contact the tour POC.

Parking:

  • Building 4619: Rideout Rd/West parking lot (tours starting with Experimental Fluids and Environmental Test branch) or Beacon St/North parking lot (tours starting with Structural Strength or Structural Dynamics Test branches)
  • Building 4666: Dodd Rd/North parking lot, or back of building parking lot if North lot is full. DO NOT enter and attempt to park in the lot off Saturn Rd — this belongs to Blue Origin.
  • East or West Test Areas: Saturn Rd lot, near East Test area entrance. Note: Only your Tour Guide can badge through the East or West Test area gates.

Attendance: Visitors should not expect to leave their vehicle in the lot and ride along with the guide unless otherwise instructed or arranged. If you are riding with your Tour Guide, please expect to STOP at the East or West TA gate to be "badged through" by your Tour Guide.

Be prepared for: Loud noises, unconditioned areas, and uneven, rocky, or muddy terrain.

Required apparel/PPE: Closed-toe shoes and long pants are required. East and West test area tours are primarily outdoors — sunblock, sunglasses, and hats are recommended.

Photography: Do NOT photograph any Blue Origin assets in any area. Do NOT photograph any FBI or ARMY RANGE if you are on top of 4693. Ask your Tour Guide before taking photos. Do not post photos on social media without permission.

For reviewers

Project Brief — Test Lab Self-Guided Tour

The thinking behind the prototype: the concept, the decisions and why, the roadmap, and what research says makes a tour great. The interactive prototype is the Home / Stops / Contact tabs; this brief is the plan.

How to review: (1) Click through the prototype — Home → Stops → Flat Floor → Contact; try Listen, the quiz, and the video. (2) Read this brief. (3) Anything marked “placeholder” is illustrative — real media and content go through clearance. Full write-ups live in the project folder’s .md files (listed at the end).

1 · The concept

  • What: a self-guided tour visitors reach by QR at each stop (or on a loaner tablet) — facts, images, video, and a way to contact us.
  • Two goals at once: educate (the engineering) and advertise (our testing services). Every stop carries a “Work with the Test Lab” call-to-action.
  • One repository, many outputs: the same cleared content feeds the on-site tour and static presentations for captive audiences. Build the content once; render it many ways.

2 · Key decisions & why

D4One content model → many renderers (tablet, web, presentations)The tour and the presentations are the same content in different contextsDecided

D7Prefer already-public NASA sources (NTRS, nasa.gov, images.nasa.gov) as cleared inputsNASA already made the release call by publishing — lightens our reviewDecided

3 · Design principles (12)

  • 1 · Content is data, not code — one repository, two products
  • 2 · Static-first, server-light — minimize attack surface & cost
  • 3 · Clearance before publish — nothing ships un-reviewed
  • 4 · Accessibility is the floor (Section 508 / WCAG 2.1 AA)
  • 5 · Works on the worst phone, worst signal
  • 6 · Maintainable by a non-developer — design for handoff
  • 7 · Dual-purpose by construction — educate and advertise
  • 8 · Approved resources only (NASAWDS, images.nasa.gov, official video)
  • 9 · Engage through progressive disclosure, not volume
  • 10 · Measure intent, respect privacy — anonymous, no PII
  • 11 · Story over inventory — don’t let the database shape the tour
  • 12 · The screen serves the room — point attention to the real hardware

4 · Roadmap

The critical path is governance & people, not software. The build is the easy part. This fails — if it fails — because content can’t get cleared, no hosting path materializes, or maintenance collapses at handoff. So discovery is front-loaded and the build sits downstream of it.
Phase 0 Discovery & Governance ───────────────┐ (gates everything public-facing) │ Phase 1 Content Model & Store ── partly ────┤ (can start before Phase 0 closes) parallel to Phase 0 ▼ Phase 2 Single-Stop Pilot (Flat Floor) ── first live test of the clearance gate ▼ Phase 3 Evaluate ── GO / NO-GO ─────────────┐ ▼ Phase 4 Scale content + renderers Phase 5 Handoff & Sustainment (alongside 4)
  • Phase 0 — Discovery & Governance: find the public-release authority + turnaround, the Comms/OCIO hosting path, and how an approval is recorded. Critical path.
  • Phase 1 — Content model & store: stand up the SharePoint List with the clearance fields; design a simple publish step. May overlap Phase 0.
  • Phase 2 — Single-stop pilot (Flat Floor): author from already-public sources, run it through the real clearance process, load the iPad, and walk the actual stop to test connectivity + UX.
  • Phase 3 — Evaluate: explicit GO/NO-GO before scaling.
  • Phase 4 — Scale: remaining stops + the presentation renderer + (if a path exists) public web reach.
  • Phase 5 — Handoff & sustainment: document the publish step where it lives; a <30-min, no-developer update path; clearance re-review cadence.

5 · What makes a great tour (research)

Benchmark to copy: Bloomberg Connects — the free digital guide behind 1,500+ museums. Praised for exactly our constraints: downloadable offline guides, audio/video/text mix, accessibility built in (VoiceOver, captions, transcripts, zoom, font scaling, translation), a no-download route, and wayfinding maps. For space storytelling, Kennedy Space Center (Atlantis / Shuttle Launch Experience) is the model — it makes engineering feel like a mission.

What the winners do

  • Story over inventory — people, place, purpose, plot; emotion, not a spec catalog.
  • Guide, don’t dump — a clear path beats a wall of options.
  • Give the visitor a goal — a thing to find/answer raises dwell time. (→ the quiz on each stop.)
  • Design for sound-off — captions/text-first; serves engagement and 508.
  • Audio is the on-site superpower — lets eyes stay on the real hardware. (→ the “Listen” player.)
  • Offline + no-download — exactly our model (validates the offline tablet).
  • Wayfinding — visitors get lost; orientation matters.

The honest counter-evidence

Nielsen Norman Group: virtual tours have “high interaction cost, moderate usefulness,” and in real estate 82% still said in-person was decisive. But that’s the case against remote tours — our job is the inverse and the strong one: we augment a real visit, not replace it. North-star that follows: the screen serves the room — the device points attention outward to the real hardware, never competing with it. And avoid over-immersion (gyroscope AR / fast 360 pans are nauseating).
