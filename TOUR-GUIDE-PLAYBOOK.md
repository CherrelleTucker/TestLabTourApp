# Tour Guide Playbook

**Practical guidance for hosting facility tours at NASA Marshall**  
**Source**: Lessons from 2024 Summer of Engineering Tours (13 weeks, 12 buildings, 0 budget)

---

## Quick Reference: Tour Model Selection

| Building Type | Recommended Model | Tour Length | Group Size | Key Success Factor |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------|------------|-------------------|
| **1-2 easy-access labs** | Continuous | 10-15 min | 15-25 | Multiple guides |
| **Long building, many labs** | Pairs | 15 min/lab | 15 | Groups know next destination |
| **Compact, many labs** | Hub (with caution) | 30 min | 10-15 | Short walking distances |
| **Complex, many labs** | Rotation | 10 min/lab | 25 | Heavy logistics support |
| **Remote/parking-limited** | Van Shuttle | 20-30 min | 10/van | Dedicated pickup & dropoff vans |

---

## Core Principles

### ✅ What Makes a Successful Tour Stop

**Multiple tour guides per lab** (minimum 2)
- One guide going long doesn't upend the entire schedule
- Backup if primary guide has an emergency
- Zero fault tolerance = recipe for disaster

**Volunteers for logistics**
- Stragglers, parking direction, security escort
- **Example**: 4602 success relied on 10 interns for logistics + 2 guides per lab

**Flexibility in tour model**
- No one-size-fits-all approach
- Choose based on building layout, lab count, and expected demand
- What worked in Building 4711 (compact, hub model) failed in Building 4619 (long, hub model)

**Advance coordination**
- **Security**: Door prop approvals, escort procedures
- **Operations**: Test schedules (avoid surprises)
- **Facilities**: Parking maps, overflow areas, signage

---

### ❌ What to Avoid

**Single tour guide**
- Zero fault tolerance if guide is sick/emergency
- Tour going long cascades to all following tours

**Hub model in long buildings**
- Central queuing works for compact spaces (Building 4711 ✅)
- Fails in long layouts due to excessive walking (Building 4619 ❌)

**Unrestricted signups**
- Power users sign up for every slot, crowding out others
- **Fix**: Limit to 1-2 tour pairs per person (used successfully in 4205)

**Over-long series**
- 13 weeks = attendance waned by week 10
- **Recommendation**: 4–8 weeks max

**2-hour commitment tours**
- Rotation model (Building 4602) = slots filled slowly
- High barrier to entry, lower participation
- Only justified if seeing all labs is essential

## Tour Models: When to Use Each

### 1. **Continuous Model** 

**Best For**: 1-2 labs, easy access, high demand  
**Tour Flow**: Small groups start every X minutes, no group dependencies  
**Example**: Building 4755 (ECLSS, Friction Stir Welding)

**Typical Setup**:
- Tour length: 10-15 minutes
- Start interval: Every 15 minutes
- Group size: 15-25 people
- Duration: 1-2 hours (can extend if demand is high)

✅ **Advantages**:
- Simple logistics
- No designated tour groups needed
- Easy to extend if demand is high

❌ **Challenges**:
- Requires multiple guides to keep on schedule
- High-demand stops fill quickly

**Real Example — Building 4755 (2024)**:
- Tour slots filled in 5 hours
- Extended to 2nd hour to accommodate demand
- Multiple tour guides kept things on time
- **Result**: Very successful ✅

---

### 2. **Pairs Model**

**Best For**: Long buildings with multiple labs  
**Tour Flow**: Two labs paired (A/B), groups swap after 15 min, new batch arrives at interval  
**Example**: Building 4205 (NTREES/Solid Prop, Green Prop/CFM, SIL/TVC)

**Typical Setup**:
- Tour length: 15 minutes per lab (30 min total commitment)
- Start interval: Every 15 minutes
- Group size: 15 people
- Lab pairs: 2 labs per pairing
- Participants: Limited to signing up for ONE tour pair

**How It Works**:
1. Group A starts at Lab 1, Group B starts at Lab 2
2. At 15-minute mark, groups swap
3. At 30-minute mark, Groups A & B depart, Groups C & D arrive
4. Groups C & D swap after 15 minutes
5. Repeat

✅ **Advantages**:
- One tour going long does NOT upend entire stop
- Event host does less people-wrangling (groups know next destination)
- Works well in long buildings where hub model would require excessive walking

❌ **Challenges**:
- People want to see more labs (limited to one pair)
- Requires coordination between paired labs

**Real Example — Building 4205 (2024)**:
- 3 lab pairs offered
- Participants limited to signing up for one pair
- **Result**: Logistically successful ✅
- One tour running long didn't disrupt other pairs

**Real Example — Building 4707 (2024)**:
- Additive Manufacturing/3D Scanning paired with Water Blast/Paint Booth, Composites/CT Scanning
- **Result**: Pairs model worked well in long building ✅

---

### 3. **Hub Model**

**Best For**: Compact buildings with multiple labs (use with caution)  
**Tour Flow**: Central queuing point, guides fetch groups and return them  
**Example**: Building 4711 (compact, 30-min tours ✅) vs Building 4619 (long, 15-min tours ❌)

**Typical Setup**:
- Tour length: 30 minutes (longer tours work better for hub)
- Start interval: Varies
- Group size: 10-15 people
- Hub location: Central lobby or common area

**How It Works**:
1. All participants queue at central hub
2. Tour guide fetches a group, brings to Lab A
3. After tour, guide returns group to hub
4. Guide fetches next group, brings to Lab B
5. Repeat

✅ **Advantages** (when building is compact):
- All participants in one known location
- Easy to communicate schedule changes
- Works well with longer tours (30+ minutes)

❌ **Challenges**:
- Excessive walking in long buildings (Building 4619 failure)
- One tour going long can cascade to following tours
- Participants spend time waiting at hub

**Real Example — Building 4619 (2024)**:
- Hub model with 15-minute tours, 4 labs, long building layout
- **Result**: Too much unnecessary walking ❌
- **Recommendation**: Should have used pairs model (TVAC/Flat Floor, Load Test Annex/Structural Dynamics)

**Real Example — Building 4711 (2024)**:
- Hub model with 30-minute tours, 5 labs, compact building
- **Result**: Hub worked better in condensed building with longer tours ✅
- Contrast to 4619 shows importance of matching model to building layout

---

### 4. **Rotation Model**

**Best For**: When participants MUST see all labs (use sparingly)  
**Tour Flow**: Fixed groups rotate through all labs in sequence (2-hour commitment)  
**Example**: Building 4602 (6 materials labs)

**Typical Setup**:
- Tour length: 10 minutes per lab
- Start interval: Every 15 minutes
- Group size: 25 people per group
- Groups: Color-coded (Yellow, Blue, Green, Purple)
- Duration: 2 hours (full rotation)

**How It Works**:
1. Each group assigned a color and starting lab
2. Groups rotate through all labs on fixed schedule
3. All groups see all labs by end of 2 hours

✅ **Advantages**:
- Ensures every participant sees every lab
- Comprehensive experience

❌ **Challenges**:
- **High logistics burden**: Requires extensive pre-planning
- **2-hour commitment**: Slots filled more slowly
- **Zero fault tolerance**: Complex coordination required
- **Heavy staffing needs**: Building 4602 required ~10 interns for logistics + 2 guides per lab

**Real Example — Building 4602 (2024)**:
- 6 labs (Chemistry, Mechanical Test, Microscopy, SEM, Rad Labs, Alloy Development)
- 4 color-coded groups (Yellow, Blue, Green, Purple)
- 25 people per group
- **Success factor**: EM provided ~10 interns for logistics + 2 tour guides per lab
- **Result**: Difficult but successful ⚠️
- **Note**: "This tour stop had great potential for disaster, but EM's pre-planning was essential to tour stop success"

**Recommendation**: Only use rotation model if you have logistics support at the level of Building 4602. Otherwise, use pairs model and accept that participants will see fewer labs.

---

### 5. **Van Shuttle Model**

**Best For**: Off-site stops, parking-constrained buildings  
**Tour Flow**: Vans ferry participants to/from tour location  
**Examples**: Building 4605 (small parking lot), Building 4666 (West Test Area)

**Typical Setup**:
- Tour length: 10-30 minutes
- Group size: 10 people per van
- Vans: 2+ (one for pickup, one for dropoff)
- Drivers: 1 per van
- Tour guides: 1 per van or per lab

**How It Works**:
1. Participants meet at staging area (e.g., Building 4601 for 4605 tour)
2. Van 1 picks up Group A, drives to tour location
3. While Group A tours, Van 2 picks up Group B
4. Van 1 returns Group A to staging area, picks up Group C
5. Repeat

✅ **Advantages**:
- Solves parking constraints
- Tours going long don't disrupt next pickup (if using dedicated pickup/dropoff vans)
- Enables access to remote facilities

❌ **Challenges**:
- Requires vehicle resources (vans, drivers)
- Weather-dependent for outdoor stops
- Coordination complexity

**Real Example — Building 4605 (2024)**:
- Small parking lot at 4605
- Participants met at 4601 lobby
- 2 vans: one dedicated to pickups, one to dropoffs
- **Key innovation**: Separate pickup/dropoff vans meant tours going long didn't disrupt next tours
- Linear tour progression: Lobby → PLANET → HISET (clears lobby for next group)
- **Result**: Logistically challenging but successful ⚠️

**Real Example — Building 4666 West Test Area (2024)**:
- Driving tour of East/West Test Areas
- 2 vans, 10 tourists + 1 driver + 1 guide per van
- 20-minute tours every 20 minutes
- ET10/ET50 displays in 4666 lobby
- **Result**: Easy, fun tour ✅
- **Critical lesson**: Check Blue Origin test schedule week-of (West TA unexpectedly off-limits day-of due to testing)

### Building 4619 (Structural Test Lab)

**Labs**: Flat Floor, Structural Test Stands, TVAC, Load Test Annex, Structural Dynamics

**2024 Model Used**: Hub ❌  
**Recommended Model**: **Pairs**

**Suggested Pairings**:
- Pair 1: TVAC + Flat Floor
- Pair 2: Load Test Annex + Structural Dynamics

**Tour Timing**:
- 10-15 minutes per lab
- 15 people per group

**Lessons**:
- Hub model failed due to long building layout (too much walking)

**Recommended Model**: **Continuous**

**Tour Timing**:
- 10 minutes per tour
- Start every 15 minutes
- 15 people per tour

- Great tour to start with (very easy)

- Designated tour groups unnecessary
- Multiple tour guides necessary to keep tours on time

**2024 Model Used**: Pairs ✅

**Suggested Pairings**:
- Pair 1: NTREES + Solid Prop Lab
- Pair 2: Green Prop Lab + CFM Lab
- Pair 3: SIL/SITF + TVC Lab

**Tour Timing**:
- 10-15 minutes per lab (30 min per pair)

**Lessons**:

## Major Operational Takeaways

### Tour Timing Recommendations

| Tour Duration | Best For | Participant Commitment | Fill Rate | Notes |
|---------------|----------|------------------------|-----------|-------|
| **10-15 min** | Easy, high-demand stops | Low (drop-in style) | Fast | Default recommendation |
| **30 min** | Medium complexity, pairs | Medium | Medium | Works for compact buildings (hub) or pairs |
| **1 hour** | Multiple labs (pairs/hub) | Medium-High | Medium | Pairs model with 2 labs/pair |
| **2 hours** | Full rotation (many labs) | High | Slow | Only if logistics support available |

**General Recommendation**: 
- Default to 10-15 min tours starting every 15 min
- Extend to 30 min for complex labs or pairs model
- Avoid 2+ hour commitments unless exceptional circumstances

**Participant Feedback**:
- "Most common complaint: short tour duration"
- **However**: "short tours mean more tours" (higher capacity, more participants)
- Balance: Longer tours = fewer slots = lower participation

---

### Success Metrics

✅ **Attendance ≠ Success**
- Building 4705 (Neutral Buoyancy Simulator): one of least-attended stops
- **Result**: Led to partnership between 7-axis milling machine shop and 3D scanning lab for Orion heat shield work
- **Takeaway**: Focus on collaboration facilitation, not just headcount

✅ **Broad Appeal**
- No dominant demographic: interns, early-career, late-career, mid-career all participated
- Cross-center participation: MAF, GSFC, KSC all had participants

✅ **Budget**
- Entire 13-week, 12-building series cost $0
- Demonstrates feasibility without funding

❌ **Series Too Long**
- Attendance waned over 13 weeks
- Recommendation: 4–8 weeks max

## Pre-Tour Checklist

### 3 Months Before (Event Planning)

- [ ] Present idea to directorate leadership (ED01, etc.)
- [ ] Present to department managers
- [ ] Identify building POCs from department managers
- [ ] Hold kickoff meeting with all building POCs

### 2-3 Weeks Before Each Stop

- [ ] Hold individual meeting with building POC and tour guides for initial planning
- [ ] Confirm tour model (continuous, pairs, hub, rotation, van shuttle)
- [ ] Determine tour timing, group size, duration
- [ ] Coordinate with security (door props, escort procedures, access restrictions)
- [ ] Coordinate with operations (test schedules, equipment availability)
- [ ] Coordinate with facilities (parking maps, overflow parking, signage)
- [ ] Identify volunteer logistics support if needed
- [ ] Create signup sheet (PowerApps preferred over spreadsheet)

### 1 Week Before Each Stop

- [ ] Send signup sheet to target audience
- [ ] Include parking map and wayfinding instructions
- [ ] Include building access requirements (escort, security, PPE)
- [ ] Post signup in Teams channels and distribute to Center AOs
- [ ] Monitor signup rate and adjust (extend hours, open 2nd session, etc.)

### Day Before Each Stop

- [ ] Hold final planning meeting with building POC and tour guides
- [ ] Review participant list and group assignments (if applicable)
- [ ] Confirm logistics volunteers are available
- [ ] Double-check operations (test schedules, equipment status)
- [ ] Prepare signage for parking, wayfinding, pedestrian gates
- [ ] Verify security clearances and escort availability

### Day Of

- [ ] Post volunteers in parking lots to direct overflow
- [ ] Post signage on pedestrian gates (for buildings behind vehicle gates)
- [ ] Set up hub/staging area (if applicable)
- [ ] Brief all tour guides and volunteers on schedule
- [ ] Have backup plan for tours running long
- [ ] Monitor tours for stragglers and timing issues

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Tour running long threatens entire schedule

**Solutions**:
- Use pairs model instead of hub (one tour delay doesn't cascade)
- Have minimum 2 tour guides per lab (one slow guide doesn't bottleneck)
- Build in buffer time between groups
- Have volunteer logistics support to manage stragglers

---

### Problem: Parking lot fills up, people park on grass

**Solutions**:
- Send parking map 1 week before tour
- Identify overflow parking areas
- Post volunteer in parking lot to direct traffic
- Use NASA banner flags to mark overflow areas (worked well at 4666)

---

### Problem: People can't find building (especially behind vehicle gates)

**Solutions**:
- Post extra signs on pedestrian turnstiles/fences
- Include detailed wayfinding in signup instructions
- Send map with building highlighted
- Post volunteer at entry points to direct visitors

---

### Problem: Unrestricted signups crowd out others

**Solutions**:
- Limit signups to 1-2 tour pairs per person (4205 model)
- Use PowerApps instead of spreadsheet (enforces limits, prevents editing)
- Stagger signup opening (VIP/coordinators first, then general)

---

### Problem: Operations surprise (test schedule conflict, equipment down)

**Solutions**:
- Check test schedule week-of event (Blue Origin conflict at 4666)
- Build relationships with operations POCs
- Have backup lab or display option
- Communicate changes to participants in advance

---

### Problem: Secured doors lock out participants

**Solutions**:
- Call security ahead of time to get permission to prop open doors (4205 lesson)
- Arrange escort coverage for full tour duration
- Have backup access method (key card, volunteer escort)

---

### Problem: 2-hour commitment = low signups

**Solutions**:
- Use pairs model instead of rotation (30 min commitment vs 2 hours)
- Accept that participants will see fewer labs (quality over quantity)
- Only use rotation if logistics support is available (4602 level)
