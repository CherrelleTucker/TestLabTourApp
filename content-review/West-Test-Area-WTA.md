### [FULL CONTENT INCLUDED IN ET10-PROPULSION-TEST-LAB.md]

**Summary for WTA Context:**
- **Status:** Historic site (last F-1 firing: February 13, 1969)
- **Built:** 1961-1964
- **Purpose:** Single-engine F-1 testing, including combustion instability "bomb drop" tests
- **Significance:** Proven every F-1 engine before it was clustered for Saturn V
- **Tour Time:** ~20 min

**Key Stat:** 1.5M lbf per engine tested

### [FULL CONTENT INCLUDED IN ET10-PROPULSION-TEST-LAB.md]

**Summary for WTA Context:**
- **Status:** Active facility, still in Marshall's test inventory

## Contextual Information for All WTA Stops

### Shared Story Elements

These themes connect WTA facilities and should be woven through all WTA stop content:

#### 1. Scale of Testing
- WTA handles the largest propulsion test articles at Marshall
- From single engines (4696) to full stages (4670)
- Infrastructure built to withstand millions of pounds of thrust

#### 2. Heritage and Continuity
- WTA test methods proven in Saturn era still used today
- Facilities outlast individual programs (Saturn → Shuttle → SLS)
- Institutional knowledge preserved through decades of operation

#### 3. Ground-Based Validation
- Critical philosophy: prove it here before it flies
- Static testing catches problems that would doom missions
- Economics: cheaper to fail on the ground than in flight

#### 4. Engineering at Extremes
- Keying structures into bedrock
- Water deluge systems (136,000 gal/min for F-1)
- Flame deflectors designed to survive repeated firings
- Instrumentation to capture data during violent testing

## "Look For" Suggestions Across WTA

If tour groups see multiple WTA facilities, these connecting themes help:

### Physical Scale
"Notice how the stands are built into the ground, not just on top of it. Test Stand 4670 is engineered so the ground wins the fight against millions of pounds of thrust."

### Infrastructure
"Look for the flame deflector channels cut into the ground - those water-cooled chutes have to survive exhaust hotter than a blowtorch, test after test."

### Historic vs. Active
"Compare the F-1 stand, quiet since 1969, with Test Stand 4670, still in active use. Same test philosophy, same WTA, different generations of hardware."

### Safety Distance
"Notice how far back the control buildings are from the stands. When you're firing millions of pounds of thrust, you don't stand close."
