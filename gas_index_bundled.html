5 Â· What makes a great tour (research)

Benchmark to copy: Bloomberg Connects â€” the free digital guide behind 1,500+ museums. Praised for exactly our constraints: downloadable offline guides, audio/video/text mix, accessibility built in (VoiceOver, captions, transcripts, zoom, font scaling, translation), a no-download route, and wayfinding maps. For space storytelling, Kennedy Space Center (Atlantis / Shuttle Launch Experience) is the model â€” it makes engineering feel like a mission.

What the winners do

  • Story over inventory â€” people, place, purpose, plot; emotion, not a spec catalog.
  • Guide, don't dump â€” a clear path beats a wall of options.
  • Give the visitor a goal â€” a thing to find/answer raises dwell time. (â†’ the quiz on each stop.)
  • Design for sound-off â€” captions/text-first; serves engagement and 508.

  • Offline + no-download â€” exactly our model (validates the offline tablet).

The honest counter-evidence

But that's the case against remote tours â€” our job is the inverse and the strong one: we augment a real visit, not replace it. North-star that follows: the screen serves the room â€” the device points attention outward to the real hardware, never competing with it. And avoid over-immersion (gyroscope AR / fast 360 pans are nauseating).
